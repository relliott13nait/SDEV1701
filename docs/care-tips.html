How to Plant Peonies

Fall planting (before the first frost) ensures strong root establishment and blooms the next spring, here are some main tips:

  • Spring-planted peonies may take 2+ years to bloom.
  • Pick a well-draining spot with at least 6 hours of sun per day.
  • Avoid planting near large trees or shrubs to prevent root competition.

Planting Depth Guide

  • Herbaceous Peonies: "Eyes" (buds) should be ½ inch below the soil in warm zones, 2 inches in cold zones.
  • Tree Peonies: Plant the graft 4 to 6 inches deep to encourage strong shrub growth.
  • Itoh Peonies: Position ½ inch to 1.5 inches deep, depending on climate.

Growing Tips

Even the most resilient plants face occasional growing challenges, and peonies are no exception. If you’ve ever wondered, "Why aren’t my peonies blooming?" or "What are these ants doing on my flowers?", you’re not alone. Understanding common issues and how to fix them will help you grow healthier, stronger plants.

Growing Peonies in Pots

  • Use a large pot with drainage holes.
  • Water frequently but avoid soggy soil.
  • In cold climates, bring pots indoors for winter protection.

Peony Care & Maintenance

Many problems stem from improper planting depth, lack of sunlight, or incorrect watering habits. Other issues, such as leaf curl or fungal infections, can arise due to environmental stress or poor air circulation. The good news? Most peony problems have simple solutions that can restore your plants to full health.

  • Peonies need deep, weekly watering in dry months (about 1 inch of water).
  • Overwatering can lead to root rot—ensure proper drainage.
  • Herbaceous and Intersectional Peonies: Cut back to the ground in fall to prevent disease.
  • Tree Peonies: Avoid heavy pruning—only remove dead or damaged branches.
  • Botrytis blight and powdery mildew can occur with poor air circulation.
  • Keep plants well-spaced and remove any affected leaves immediately.
  • Staking helps support heavy flowers in rainy climates.
